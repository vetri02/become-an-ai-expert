### Answer key: Module 17

### Quiz answers and reasoning

1. Own a problem end to end (evidence: your manager's one sentence); known outside the company (a star, citation or invitation from a stranger); arbitrarily deep under questioning (a recorded hour on one topic); learning on a cadence (dated eval re-runs and one long study). Any marker whose only evidence is your own opinion is the weakest.
2. Pin the model ID; re-run capability evals with the new model's own best prompt; re-run safety, injection and tool-abuse suites; measure noise by running the incumbent twice; compare per category against the noise band; canary on sampled traffic; re-baseline the regression layer after migration.
3. Reporting compresses and often inverts what a lab post or paper says; the primary source is short enough to read and is what you will be asked about. The practitioners who keep up well read model cards and engineering posts directly.
4. It helps with filters and some hiring managers admit bias; it is not required to do the work; if you can get it paid for after you are employed, it is worth having; it will teach less than the projects did.
5. Copy the adjacent-job-then-sideways route that nine of the profiled people took, not the dropout-to-lab route that three famous people took; the famous cases are visible because they are rare.

### Lab assessment and mastery gate

The reviewer asks for the dated checklist results on a real system and reads the plan for a named artifact and date. Vague plans fail.
