### Answer key: Module 16

### Quiz answers and reasoning

1. Research time is the scarce resource and most directions fail; short loops reveal which ones have signal cheaply. Pivot when a week of work has produced no surprise and no measurable movement, or when the result would not change what you believe either way.
2. It gives no baseline noise, folds capability and general ability into one number, and omits the settings and the seed count. Lab 13's noise band and Module 06's eval table are the minimum; "0.41 to 0.57 on three seeds, noise 0.03, general ability unchanged within noise, KL 0.05, group 8" is a result.
3. Whether the group of samples for a prompt contains both successes and failures; if variance is zero the prompt gives no gradient (all pass or all fail). Loss in RL is not a progress metric; reward variance tells you which prompts are still teaching the model.
4. Because the field's filter against them means a clear negative result is rare and useful, it saves others repeating dead ends, and reviewers can tell an honest ablation from a demo by whether the failures are reported.
5. Externally reviewed output, then a strong first-author post, then nothing. So the next six months should produce one piece reviewed by someone outside your circle, posted where the field reads, not five private notebooks.
6. One variable changed, everything else fixed including seeds and data order, enough runs to estimate noise, and the same evaluation for every setting. Changing two things, cherry-picking seeds, or evaluating settings on different data breaks it.

### Lab assessment and mastery gate

The reviewer asks for the external review in writing and checks the ablation changed one variable. A results table with only the winning setting fails.
