### Answer key: Module 12

### Quiz answers and reasoning

1. A named business problem with a dollar cost; one tool and a 30-day scope under a few hundred dollars; the data-security and vendor-risk questions answered up front. "Let me learn AI" with no problem, no cost and no scope gets rejected.
2. Your transferable skills are serving, pipelines, monitoring and eval infrastructure, which AI teams need and often lack; framing the move around model training competes with PhDs on their strongest ground and discards your advantage.
3. The result, with a number: "recall@5 0.84 on a 60-question golden set, $0.004 per query, p95 1.2 s." Not the technology list.
4. It is low-risk, obviously useful, shows you read the code (you found the gap between the registry and the docs), and gets reviewed quickly, which starts the relationship with maintainers.
5. No stated accuracy or baseline; no failure discussion; no live URL or one-command run; course exercises presented as projects; unclear what part is yours.
6. Read the seminal papers of one subfield, implemented and open-sourced a project, made videos, wrote a summary post. It worked because each block produced public artifacts that people in that subfield could find and cite, which is what produced the referral.

### Lab assessment and mastery gate

The reviewer looks for the external signal (a star, citation or comment from a stranger) and traces one number in the results memo. A pilot without eval numbers fails the gate regardless of how well it was received.

### How to use this key

Open after the quiz. Question 1 is the pitch you will give; rehearse it aloud.
